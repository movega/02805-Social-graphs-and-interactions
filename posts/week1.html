Week 01 Components & degree

The frontier of the Marvel universe

Everybody looks at Spider-Man. We went the other way, to the edge of the map: the seventeen characters with no links at all, the island of nine that never speaks to anyone, and the eighteen holding on by a single thread.

What we asked

The obvious week-1 question is who sits at the centre. Spider-Man is linked to by 106 of the other 302 articles, and the answer stops being interesting about ten seconds after you compute it.

So we asked the opposite question, and it turned out to be the more awkward one: what does it actually take to be inside Wikipedia's Marvel universe? The category says 303 characters belong. The link structure disagrees — it quietly leaves twenty-six of them outside, and holds another eighteen by a single thread.

What we did

  1. Loaded the frozen week-1 release roster first, edges second. Build the graph from the edge list alone and the 17 characters with no link in either direction vanish without an error message — they are exactly the population this post is about.
  2. Split the network into connected components and looked at everything that was not the giant one.
  3. Read the opening sentence of all 17 castaway articles, because at that point the data had run out and the answer was in the prose.
  4. Measured which way the links point at the edge of the network, and compared it with the centre.
  5. Went to the live Wikipedia API to try to disprove two things: that the isolation is real rather than a harvesting artefact, and that it is not simply explained by "nobody finished writing those articles".

The map

Nineteen components: one continent of 277, one island of 9, and seventeen people in the water. There is no archipelago in between — no cluster of three, no pair holding hands. You are either in the universe or entirely out of it.

The island: nine characters who only talk to each other

The second component is nine characters — Backhand, Blackthorn, Radian, Scaredycat, Scatterbrain, Shear, Snapdragon, Toxyn and Vyking — with 22 links among them and not one leaving. Internally they are denser than the network at large. Externally they do not exist.

All nine come from Strikeforce: Morituri, a 1986–89 series set in its own continuity. Three of them carry "(Morituri)" in the article title. Nobody in that story ever meets Spider-Man, so no editor ever had a reason to write the link.

The obvious rival explanation is that these are stubs with no links in them at all. The wiki-source says otherwise:

They link out plenty — to the series, the writers, the publisher, the plot. The last column is the finding: zero, nine times out of nine. The island is not a gap in the data. It is a narrative border that survived intact into Wikipedia's link structure.

The seventeen castaways are not one group

The isolates have nothing in common as characters. Read their first sentences and they sort themselves into four kinds of outsider:

Being isolated is not the same as being minor. Baymax headlines a Disney film and has not a single link in either direction.

The obvious objection, and how far it gets

"They are stubs." That is a real explanation and it deserved a real test, so we pulled the byte size of all 303 articles from the API.

It is largely right. Article size and degree correlate at ρ = 0.82 across the roster, the castaways' median article is 6.7 kB against 18.0 kB for the connected ones, and the island's articles are smaller still at 4.9 kB. If we had stopped here, the honest headline would have been short articles get few links.

Two castaways refuse to fit. Miracleman is a 62 kB article with 440 [[links]] in its wiki-source and zero of them landing on another Marvel superhero. Baymax is 24 kB with 120 links and the same zero. For comparison, connected characters with articles in the 20–70 kB band have a median degree of 11 (n = 91). These two have 0.

Both are exactly the side-door cases from the table above: a character Marvel acquired from another publisher, and a character who arrived from a film. Article length explains the bulk of the rim. It does not explain the doorway.

What surprised us: the rim reaches in, the centre never reaches back

Eighteen characters hang off the giant component by a single link. We expected the direction of those links to be a coin flip. It is not:

  • 15 of the 18 only link out. One is only linked to. Two go both ways.
  • Their anchors repeat: five of them hold on to Spider-Man, two to Doctor Strange.

So we checked whether this is a property of the whole network rather than a coincidence in eighteen cases. For every character we computed a polarity: (in − out) / (in + out), which is −1 for someone who only links out and +1 for someone who is only linked to.

The gradient is clean. The periphery writes about the centre; the centre does not write back. And it makes editorial sense once you say it out loud: when you write the article for a minor character, you have to explain who they are, which means naming the famous people they fought or joined. When you write Spider-Man, you cannot name everyone — 700 [[links]] in his wiki-source and only 9 of them land on another character in this category.

That is also why the rim is so heavily populated. 92 of the 303 characters — 30% of the roster — have three links or fewer.

Checking the snapshot against live Wikipedia

Every claim above rests on the frozen snapshot, and "this character has no links" is exactly the kind of claim a harvesting bug would produce. Internal links live in the wiki-source as [[Page name]], which is how the course built the edge list, so we re-harvested a few articles ourselves and compared.

The snapshot holds. The castaways are genuinely uncited by their own universe, and Spider-Man's nine outgoing links are really nine.

Wikipedia's Marvel universe is not held together by who matters. It is held together by who had a reason to write the sentence.

Caveats

  • This is a network of articles, not of characters. An edge means an editor wrote a link, which tracks fame, article length and editorial habit at least as much as it tracks the stories.
  • The category boundary is doing a lot of work. Every "0" in the last column of the island table means "no link to another page inside this 303-character category" — those articles link to plenty of Marvel pages that are not superheroes.
  • The four-way split of the castaways is our reading of seventeen opening sentences, not a measurement. Someone else might group them differently.
  • ρ = 0.82 between size and degree is a correlation with no direction attached. Long articles may attract links, or getting linked may be what makes an article grow.
  • Polarity is undefined for the castaways (0/0), so they are absent from Fig. 3 by construction.

Reproduce it

Data: the week-1 release from the course data page, frozen 2026-08-26 — 303 nodes, 1,784 directed edges. Everything in this post comes out of one script:

python analysis_week1.py          # the snapshot: figures and numbers
python analysis_week1.py --wiki   # plus the live Wikipedia API checks

Built with pandas, NetworkX and Matplotlib. The API calls send a User-Agent naming the client, because Wikipedia answers 403 without one — and back off on 429, because it answers that too if you go too fast.
